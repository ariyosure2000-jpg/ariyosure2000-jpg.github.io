Who I Am
Nurse. Technologist. Builder.

Two identities, one mission. I don’t choose between nursing and technology — I use both to fight for better healthcare.

I am a triple-certified nurse and midwife from Ekiti State, Nigeria. For three years I worked across some of Nigeria’s most demanding hospitals — managing wards where a single nurse covers 5–6 patients in rural facilities with minimal digital support, making high-stakes decisions with paper, pen, and instinct.

I watched incredible colleagues burn out — not from lack of heart, but from systems that failed them completely. Documentation done by hand. Drug calculations done mentally in the middle of emergencies. Deteriorating patients spotted by intuition alone, never by data.

I knew technology had to be the answer. So I started learning. With no formal training, no mentor, no classroom — just AI tools, Apple’s documentation, a borrowed MacBook, and five months of relentless effort — I taught myself Swift, migrated a broken, years-expired iOS codebase, resolved hundreds of compiler errors, and deployed a working build to Apple TestFlight. I am proud of what I achieved, and I am honest that AI was a major part of how I got there.

That is exactly why I am applying to the Academy. Not because I think I’m already a developer — but because I want to become one properly. I want the rigour, the mentorship, the depth, and the community that transforms passionate beginners into world-class engineers. The Academy is where my passion becomes a profession.

⭐ Real Project — Live on TestFlight
iOS Legacy Codebase — Migration & TestFlight Deployment

Uhooup is a social networking app that helps people in new environments connect with others over coffee — a platform for real-world meetups, networking, and community. A friend who had previously worked on the app offered me access to its codebase when I told her I was idle and desperate to learn tech. I had never written a line of Swift. I didn’t even own a Mac — I borrowed one. The codebase was broken, undocumented, and years out of date. Over five months, using AI tools and Apple’s documentation as my only teachers, I diagnosed and resolved hundreds of compiler errors, migrated the project to iOS 16.6+ standards, and deployed a working build to Apple TestFlight. She needed it on TestFlight. I needed to learn. It worked for both of us. This is the project that made me fall in love with Apple development — and the moment I finished it, I asked AI where I could learn properly. That search led me to the Apple Developer Academy Naples.
